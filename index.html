If you spot an error, please help fix it.

“No HPC resource” means no institutional resource — not no compute. Every US law faculty member can apply to NSF ACCESS, whose policy states that work from any field of study and funding from any source is eligible, at no cost. The entry-level tier needs only a short abstract, and the hardware behind it is frontier-class (NCSA DeltaAI: 608 GH200 superchips). Filter by the NSF ACCESS network to see the 40 schools where that is the operative route.

What this is, and what it isn't

For each US law school, this tracks whether its parent university runs a shared HPC/research-computing cluster that law faculty could plausibly use — the way a Denver Law professor can request an account on DU's Research Data Analysis Cluster, or a Northwestern Law professor can get GPU time on Quest. Standalone law schools with no parent research university are marked N/A, not "no GPUs" — there's simply nothing to check.

That institutional framing is the point, but it has a limit worth stating plainly: a grey chip on this page means your university runs nothing, not that you can't get a GPU. NSF ACCESS allocates national supercomputing time to any researcher at a US academic institution, in any field, at no cost and with no supporting grant required — a standalone law school's own email domain satisfies the affiliation requirement. Those rows are tagged NSF ACCESS in the shared-networks filter. Conversely, a bright chip doesn't guarantee you can use it: Harvard's Cannon cluster is H100/H200-class, but FAS Research Computing lists the schools it serves as FAS, SEAS, HSPH, HBS and GSE — Law is not among them. Read the caveat field before drawing conclusions from the colour.

Specs, GPU tiers, and SLURM confirmation reflect what each university's own site stated at research time — not a live feed, and not a guarantee that any specific professor gets frictionless access. Some resources are department-level rather than campus-wide, or shared systems where this particular campus's access wasn't independently confirmed; those are flagged in each school's caveat field.
